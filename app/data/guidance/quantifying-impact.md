---
title: Putting numbers in resume bullets
source: Written for ResumeFit by the project author; original text, free to reuse in this project
version: 1
---
A bullet with a number is easier to trust than one with an adjective. Useful numbers for student projects: accuracy, F1 or error on a held-out test set; size of the dataset; response time; number of users or testers; test coverage; how much time a script saved.

State what the number was measured on. "91% accuracy on a 2,000-image held-out test set" is stronger than "91% accuracy" and far stronger than "high accuracy".

Compare with a baseline when you can: "raised F1 from 0.71 (logistic regression) to 0.83". If you have no number, measure one: time your API, count your tests, run your model on a test split. Never invent or round up a figure; interviewers ask how you got it.
